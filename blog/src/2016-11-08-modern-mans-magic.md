Programming is the wizardry of the modern age. Programmers, the magic wielding arcanists; evoking mathematics, logic, and structure as if they are spells to overcome any obstacle in their path. The common man regards such skill with wide eyed wonderment as these masters of an awe inspiring trade utilize tools seemingly beyond their comprehension and wield languages foreign to their ears.

Of those who practice such syntactical sorcerers there are many levels. There are those who simply dabble in the magic, learning the words to bring forth incantations but never delving deep enough to do more than what they are given. Some can string syntax together with rudimentary structure to automate a task or uncover words within a tome of symbols. Such acolyte pursuits are honorable successes and are vital steps for every student of these profane arts of computation.

Mastery of the logical legerdemain is tricky to define and not as easily calculated as the levels of progression before it. To truly master the automated arcane, one must be able to call upon the wisdom of the unseen and predict the future to identify any problems or weaknesses a particular rote may expose This divination is not measured in the historical successes and failures of the wizard but in their propensity to draw information from all outcomes and apply it in further works. It is a state of mind, the innate gut instinct that develops from years and decades of practice, study, and no small amount of trial.

Of those with some form of mastery in the computational craft there are those who pursue the path without the innate love of it and learn for gains monetarily or in status and position. These sorcerers develop skill through the same hard work and determination as others, but were motivated by the application of such power as a means to an end, or in pursuit of some notoriety and recompense for their efforts.

The truest wizard though, and by far the most powerful of them all, is the one consumed by his craft, given over to the addictive power of the magic he wields. Spending countless hours testing the spells he wields for their most efficient solutions and uncovering hidden meaning and depth through his immense sacrifice of time. He can take seemingly unrelated disparate data and turn it into the most useful information with hidden structures designed to last centuries. He understands and learns from the spells of other wizards that came before him by pouring over their tomes and exhaustively reading the languages they used to write them.

For wizards such as this, mastery of the technological thaumaturgy is but the beginning. Though it may never amount to a fortune worthy of their drive and talent, they press on for the love of their craft, with no regrets, for a small chance to leave the art a little better than it was before. To pass on their knowledge, and in by doing so, grow the pool for others with the gift to utilize.

Through such knowledge, and by the labors of the collective works of wizards of all levels, a product is born that can take the world by storm, change lives, and even destroy nations. Such masters of the mechanical thrive in the realm of the unexplored and roam the bleeding edge of technological progress in the exploration of what is possible. There is seemingly nothing they cannot accomplish; for they are wielders of modern man's magic.
