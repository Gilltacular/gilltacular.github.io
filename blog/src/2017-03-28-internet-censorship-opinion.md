There is no doubt that censorship is a hot-button issue in this time of modern security awareness and exploitation. One side seeing it as a sort of filter for unsavory conduct and messages, while the other arguing that it stifles innovation, freedom of thought, and the very foundations of what the internet has come to be. Opinions vary but I personally do not believe the internet should be censored at all and should instead be a fully accessible resource in all its forms subject to no law, restriction, or barriers.

As Jeremy Bentham (1839), a British philosopher stated “Secrecy, being an instrument of conspiracy, ought never to be the system of regular government.” Though Bentham’s words come before the internet was even an idea in the minds of the common man, it rings true of a greater truth about what power government should have in the manipulation of access to information. It stands to reason that an internet censorship controlled by the government would be party to the same opportunity for its use as an “instrument of conspiracy” and therefore should not be available for or subject to its use.

Censorship should be a tool utilized by the individual or organization within the scope of their own choice. Web filters should be available and able to be used only as a means of shaping the types of content someone would like to receive specifically and only through their own decision to utilize it. It should never be applied as a blanket solution to access at levels that would bottleneck the flow of information from users who have not made that decision for themselves. Furthermore I believe we can treat corporations as a meta-individual in the scope of their rights to censor access to information within their grounds as well. Governments should be treated in the same way corporations are and similarly should not be able to have effect on anyone outside their offices and grounds of operation. The flow of information should not be obstructed by any entity but that of the individual person and no individual person’s choice to restrict access should affect another person's access of information.

In the instances of internet access where a crime is being committed the law should apply its power and focus toward the individuals who are committing the crime and their subsequent punishment and not toward the restriction of data. All bits should be equal in innocence and the crimes of the users should be punished for their use of the technology for nefarious purpose.

>“The freedom of a nation cannot be upheld by laws alone, but also by the light of the nation and knowledge of their use.” - Anders Chydenius

For this to be realized I believe reformation in laws and policies will need to take place to include the ideals as they exist online. Where this gets a little tricky is when such things as stalking, harassment, and abuse are pulled into the mix. What punishments should be applied to the online versions of these crimes, if any? I believe that the crime is no more or less severe because it is committed via a different medium and should be handled in the same way it would be handled in any other situation where victims and victimizers are face to face. Cyber-stalking and stalking for instance should not need alternate classification as they carry the same criminal application over different mediums. As Anders Chydenius (2006), a Swedish politician in the late 1700’s, stated “The freedom of a nation cannot be upheld by laws alone, but also by the light of the nation and knowledge of their use.” In just such a way creating new laws will not solve the problems we are facing today. All that will happen by the use of law and regulation to is the stifling of access to information and freedom of expression in this relatively new medium. Instead we should be focusing on applying the laws already in place to the crimes of the individual and not to destroy the tool being used for maleficent intent.

Thomas Jefferson, founding father father of the United States Declaration of Independence, once said “Whenever the people are well informed, they can be trusted with their own government; that whenever things get so far wrong as to attract their notice, they may be relied on to set them to rights.” Once the power of information is removed from the hands of the people and put into the interests of a government, corporation, or other controlling interest that trust cannot hold merit and we become subjects of our controller much as we were subjects of the kings and queens in the past. Adding merit to the idea of a free internet even John F. Kennedy, the thirty-fifth president of the United States, weighed in on the subject before it was ever an issue, stating that “We seek a free flow of information…we are not afraid to entrust the American people with unpleasant facts, foreign ideas, alien philosophies, and competitive values.” It is not in the spirit of censorship with which these words seem to ring. It is the freedom of information for all citizens that best resemble the ideals of these great men upon which our country has been governed. Why should today be any different?

>“Whenever the people are well informed, they can be trusted with their own government; that whenever things get so far wrong as to attract their notice, they may be relied on to set them to rights.” - Thomas Jefferson

When all bits are equally innocent and all information is allowed freedom from restriction of access, I believe we will be able to better adapt our lives to the incredible resources available to us. As individuals are left with the need to filter content, applications can be developed to better block the content they do not wish to consume. In this way technology can continue to grow to fill the needs and wants of users and access to information is not restricted in the process.
