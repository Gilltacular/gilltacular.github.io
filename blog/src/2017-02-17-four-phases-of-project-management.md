The four phases of project management are planning, organizing, monitoring and adjusting, sometimes referred to in its acronym form POMA.

In planning, a project’s requirements are weighed against the limitations of the timeframe and cost associated with the project. Though these constraints typically shape the depth of which one can extrapolate a project’s features to, they also highlight areas of risk for the project. As Tsui, Karam and Bernal state in The Essentials of Software Engineering (2014), “Having a well-conceived and documented plan, however, will help facilitate the anticipated modifications that often occur in a software project”.

In the organizing phase teams, tools, and methods are set forth that will shape the project as a whole. This phase includes planning for how you will handle the project's risks, goals, timeline, and the availability of resources. During this part of project management problems and stumbling blocks are identified and a plan is made for how they will be accounted for.

The next phase of project management is the monitoring phase. In monitoring adjustments are made to the process and planning to account for unforeseen events that may change the project’s scheduled milestones set forth in the organizing phase. Monitoring is an ongoing process in which a project manager can “regularly and continuously gauge whether the project is progressing according to plan” (Tsui et al., 2014)

The fourth and final phase of project management is the adjustment phase. In this iteration the project management team adapts the plan to account for any problems identified in the previous monitoring phase. If resources, time, or greater funding are needed this is the phase in which that is accounted for and the plan modified to accept these changes.

Reference:

Tsui, F., Karam, O., & Bernal, B. (2014). The Essentials of Software Engineering. Burlington, MA: Jones & Bartlett Learning
