Internet as a Human Right

The internet has grown to the level of necessity for most of the world. It is no longer a simple network connecting devices, but is now a global communication, expression, and information resource that is utilized in various industries essential to everyday life. Therefore access to the internet should be considered a basic human right.

The Universal Declaration of Human Rights (UDHR) sets standards for governing the achievements of humanity such that fundamental human rights remain intact. This document has remains a commitment by the governments who adopt it to the furthering of fundamental freedoms and human rights. In the 19th article of the UDHR it states:

“Everyone has the right to freedom of opinion and expression; this right includes freedom to hold opinions without interference and to seek, receive and impart information and ideas through any media and regardless of frontiers.” (The Universal Declaration of Human Rights, 1948)

Forum for the spread of information has moved to the online world. To be denied access to this world is tantamount to the censorship of freedom of opinion and the right of expression. Tim Berners-Lee, the man credited with the creation of the internet, states it is crucial to community and diversity of culture (Father of World Wide Web Says Internet Needs ‘Magna Carta’, 2014). This article alone does not stand alone in making a case for the internet as a basic human right though. The 21st and 27th articles each hold their own sway as well.

The 21st article states:

“Everyone has the right of equal access to public service in his country.” (The Universal Declaration of Human Rights, 1948).

Public service can be defined as "the business of supplying an essential commodity, such as water or electricity, or a service, such as communications or transportation, to the public." (wordnik.com, 2015). Earlier this year when the F.C.C. ruled that internet service should be regulated as a public utility (Steve Lohr, 2015) it was realized as just such an essential commodity, supporting the idea that all citizens should have a right to access internet in the same ways that they should be given access to water, or electricity.

Article 27 states:

“Everyone has the right freely to participate in the cultural life of the community to enjoy arts and to share in scientific advancement and its benefits".

How would one access those elements in a world that is increasingly moving online? The internet has become a part of the cultural life of today and scientific advancements are increasingly about or staged on the internet as well. Denial to the access is in effect limiting the breadth of both cultural life of a community and the participation and benefit of scientific advancements.

These articles, I believe, are evidence enough to showcase the importance of access to the internet as a basic human right. Access should be available to all without restriction due to its dominant and ever growing ties to culture, expression, information, business, and trade.
