Writers Mind: Reflecting on Five Weeks of Skill Development

“I should just give up on writing”. That was the most prevalent thought running through my mind as I reflected on the last weeks of composition 101 and relived that moment of being humbled before the Gods of authorship; begging for one more glimpse of the Muses. This turning point in my development stood in stark contrast to how I began. A mere five weeks ago I felt that much of what was to come would be redundant. Though I knew there was room for improvement in my work, I felt that my level of understanding far surpassed that of an introductory course; I was wrong.

"There is a great and perhaps decisive battle to be fought against ignorance, intolerance and indifference."- Murrow (1958)

Week one started with a bang, immediately we delved into concepts I had never before been exposed to such as the rhetorical triangle and an in depth analysis of Ethos, Logos, and Pathos. This alone could have filled the week with reflection over how it challenged the ways I previously had thought about my writing in the planning stages. The content did not end there. Pushing forward like a military operation intent on breaking the front lines that made up my preconceived notions, we covered topics that taught vital elements of writing such as presentation of self. This topic would later prove instrumental in creating works like the blog post you are now reading. These offerings added breadth to my experience in formatting ideas into writing styles that I had never attempted before. After week one, I knew I had misjudged this course and would be in for quite the ride in the weeks to come.

Week two was a little slower paced and sparse, but no less important to the overall development as a writer I was now experiencing. This pseudo break from the onslaught of new ideas that was week one allowed for reflection and application through discussions. It gave me practice in implementing the ideas of linguistics and proper setting. I realized just how high the bar for writing can be set when I was introduced to a quote by Edward R. Murrow in which the structure, concise terminology, and message were so expertly crafted that I had to redefine what my ultimate aspirations for my own writing looked like. Murrow writes:

This instrument can teach, it can illuminate; yes, and it can even inspire. but it can do so only to the extent that humans are determined to use it to those ends. Otherwise it is merely wires and lights in a box. There is a great and perhaps decisive battle to be fought against ignorance, intolerance and indifference. (Edward R. Murrow, address to the R.T.N.D.A. convention, October 15, 1958).

From that point on I knew what I wanted out of my writing. I knew it was not going to be as simple as taking a class or even getting a degree relevant to the topic. It was to craft images out of words that were unforgettable. To be concise and clear. I wanted to utilize the power of words to create a lasting impact on my audience. As the third week came upon me, ending just as suddenly as it began, I was surprised by just how much I had learned through the process of the previous weeks. It was as if magically without my knowledge the course material was starting to form a new author out of the ashes that the old one had become. I have never considered myself an artistic person. Creativity and abstract symbolism have always been a secondary byproduct of the structure and rules for creation that I rely upon, but I started to see myself making connections of how to utilize the structures and rules to create work that was uniquely my own. It was as if what I knew about writing was being redefined and that redefinition I found greater understanding. It raised the bar for my works as a whole. Suddenly I could see that the structures I relied upon for confidence were not merely defining characteristics of how a paper should be outlined, but were a part of the organized and point driven style with which I expressed my ideas; my voice.

In the final weeks, I found myself struggling under this new found voice to create works. It was as if everything I had learned about writing was meaningless next to the seemingly insurmountable task of how to uniquely make ideas my own. I had the outline, all the research I could ever want to support the topic, and the skills of the previous weeks before me, but I simply could not formulate any of it into an idea worth its weight. Deadlines loomed overhead and I felt as if I was the subject of a cruel joke played by the Gods of word choice, who laughed tirelessly as I creaked and croaked trying to make words with my new voice. Drafts upon drafts were created and just as easily deleted. Tens of thousands of keystrokes broke in my new keyboard in a way only months of normal use could, but still my overall works remained unworthy of the bytes they used in memory. Finally in the last hours of the week, I decided to do something unprecedented, I stopped trying to create anything. I left my computer, banned myself from my office, and tried to forget about the topics of the week. I locked the ideas of the rhetorical triangle, self presentation, formatting, proper citations, and tense in the furthest reaches of my mind. I stepped away to find clarity in the mess of confusion my over thinking the situation had caused. If my week’s experience was a downpour of rain the next morning was the fresh wet shimmering day after. The words started to trickle out as I squeezed every bit of use out of the final hours of the week. After painstakingly collecting the words that would web together my ideas, I finally had a rough draft. I finally had a moment to breathe.

"I am addicted and like any good addict I must feed my addiction ever more as my appetite grows."

I have surmounted the obstacle course of composition. I found my voice as an author and though it may still be in its infancy, I am confident that I will continue to build upon my experiences to reach new heights of expression. I am addicted and like any good addict I must feed my addiction ever more as my appetite grows. I should just give up on writing forever. I should leave the addiction behind me where it is and never look back. For the road ahead will not be an easy one, but I have accepted it already. It is a part of me and therefore the journey is fate. I am doomed to forever be set upon a path in which I can never quite reach the destination. My development as a writer is a work that will never be complete, and for the first time I am okay with that.
