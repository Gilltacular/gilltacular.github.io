My Way of Life

I am here not because I was born here, but because I was stolen away as a child and brought here. Kidnapping would be the gravest of offenses for anyone but two parents looking to avoid the high cost of living that comes along with the densely populated area of Southern California that we moved from. It was in the summer that ended the second grade in which I was ripped from all I knew and dragged hundreds of miles north to the place I was told would be my new home.

I remember cresting the hill that hides the valley below on the the highway which enters the city and thinking how strange it was, how much I hated being here even though I had just arrived. I felt like the main character in those stories you hear of children being sold into slavery, torn from all they knew to be shipped off in a metal shipping container to the unknown. Only my shipping container was a small blue Dodge van packed to the brim with my four siblings as everything we owned followed close behind in the military convoy like moving-truck we had rented earlier that week.

No matter if you are from a place, born in a place, or grew up in a place, you make that place your home little by little. With each day and dollar, with every friend and enemy you make the land changes, the climate shifts, it all uniquely becomes yours in a way that no one can take away from you. No, I have never been from here, but somehow I became of here. I grew to love this place. It is as much a part of me as I am of it.

As our little motorized wagon train moved over the mountains that enshrouded our new home, I think I knew, as much as I hated the idea, this was going to be the start of a relationship, a life. You could say that though I was not born here, that day I may have been born a second time. A new life, a new beginning, the person I would become, awaited me there. With each mile marker that person became more a part of my future.

In my town, you have not truly become a part of it until you have given up. Ownership takes you, not the other way around. The more you fight to make it yours the less you will succeed in doing so. In this way it becomes your own unique private home. It may not be the home that you knew. Perhaps like me it isn’t even the home you would have originally liked. It is better in many ways, because it is yours. This place, so unlike anything I had ever been a part of, became my home without me ever truly knowing it. Over months and years, as birthdays passed and old friends were forgotten, this place was my new home. It became where I am from.

The slower pace of life from that of the California a few hundred miles to the South is a stark contrast at first. With each thing I lost from my old home, my new home filled in ways I would have never predicted. As I gave up my love for city landscape, I realized how much I appreciated how the sun rises over trees and mountains, not desert, infrastructure, and highways. As I gave up the convenience and speed of the inner city, I instead gained new perspective on the beauty of being able to fish, hike, or just simply breath deep, without ever leaving the city limits. The breathtaking beauty of the natural world was no longer simply a diversion to my normal life, it was intricately entwined with it.

Looking back on the life I have built here, I sometimes wonder; if my old home could visit my new home, if, like me, it was stolen away without its consent and made to live here for a few years, would it ever be able to leave? Would it want to go back at all to the comforts it had? Or, like me, would it become a unique part of its new life. Like me, perhaps my old home would simply fall in love with my new home and forget that it was ever from anywhere else. Like me, perhaps my old home would also become a part of this place.
