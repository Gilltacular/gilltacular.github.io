Technology and Science

Many have sought to separate technology and culture in an attempt to define the lines of where one’s influence may affect the other. They have picked apart the examples of both and argued endlessly over the details of which came first, but in hindsight ended up no closer to their goal than when they began. I believe the reason for their lack of progress is that technology and culture have become, or perhaps always have been, inseparable from one another. They are so intrinsically intertwined that any attempt to give one superiority over another only serves to blatantly disregard the core aspects of both (Volti, 2014).

Instead of looking at technology and culture as separate entities what if we were to view them as two parts of the same mechanism for progress? What would such a construct look like? To answer these questions I find that it is best to start with an understanding of how technology and culture relate. As stated by Rudi Volti (2014), author of Societal and Technological Change,

“technology does not proceed solely through its own momentum, as implied by technological determinism; its development is strongly influenced by existing social and political arrangements”.

Those social and political arrangements mentioned in the preceding quote are the effect of culture. How they come about varies widely and is a harder mechanism to pinpoint, but the effect is the same, the progress of technology.

The relationship described in the previous paragraph does not imply that each is not its own separate entity in its own right. Culture influences technology as much as technology can influence culture and yet they remain wholly distinct. Cultural problems can be solved by technology and technological issues can be made obsolete by cultural changes. In contrast, technology may create new cultural problems and culture can in turn present new problems for technology. It is a cycle in which each part may affect the other but both hold their distinct identity separate from one another. It is only through a balance of both in a given solution that true success may be weighed. For instance, what use would a nicotine patch be without the social will to stop smoking? Alternatively, how effective would the will alone be without the aid of the nicotine patch? There are certainly instances, my own experience included, in which many have given up smoking cold turkey, but progress in medicinal technologies such as the the nicotine patch aim to help alleviate the physical burden of quitting, letting you focus more fully on overcoming the psychological binds of the habit. Each aspect of the problem is not entirely solved by either the technological or the cultural solutions presented. It is through both that the greater solution is found.

Technological solutions attempt to define clear causes of the problem in order to find an answer appropriate to the issue at hand. Cultural problems are not so easily defined. A cultural problem can contain elements of the unknown or be too diverse to nail down, such as is the case with an attempt to find a solution for crime and terrorism or the solution of obesity in the United States. In addition a cultural problem can often times be seen from many different views and therefore may be considered a problem by only a small minority of the population. This shows just how important each element is in finding a solution that works. The technological side may be able to help define a cultural problem by correlating previously unknown data into groups. That data perhaps could then be used to combat crime or obesity in a social manner which would be much more effective than either of the endeavors by themselves.

How does technology stimulate scientific discovery? The direct answer to the question of how technology stimulates scientific discovery can be found in the text Society and Technological Change when Volti states:

“Technological development also plays a vital role in scientific advance by supplying devices and instruments that are essential for scientific inquiry. From early telescopes and galvanometers to today’s electron microscopes and computers, the products of technology have steadily increased our ability to observe and analyze the phenomena that science takes as objects of inquiry.” (2014)

From this passage we can see that technological developments help provide the means with which scientific endeavors can be tested, rationalized, and realized. It is part of a cycle of progression where technology feeds scientific endeavors and those endeavors further technology which is in turn used to further science and so on. In a way technology is the tool science uses to create more technology. Another quote from Volti (2014) supports this idea when he writes

“Scientific knowledge can result in technological advances, while at the same time many technologies create both opportunities and motivations for new scientific inquiries.”

There are many examples of this throughout history. Examples where technology provided the means for which scientific discovery could be realized. Take the cathode ray tube for instance. A glass tube in which nearly all air is removed. This technology was used in the televisions and later computers but has helped science discover electrons, the atomic nucleus, x-rays, and a host of other successive technological and scientific discoveries from there (Science and Technology on Fast Forward, 2015). Without that simple cathode ray tube and the discoveries that came about from it we may have not ended up with the same progression of science and technology chain reactions that led to modern day advancements such as the x-ray, lasers, and computers at all.

Another technology that helped spur on scientific discovery was the invention of the laser. During the 1950’s its predecessor the maser was developed and led to the invention of laser technology after the inventor’s paper was published in 1958 citing that the ideas could apply to light of various spectrums. Without the first development of the maser the inventor would likely of not been able to follow the trail of findings that led him toward the success of the laser and the many technologies that utilize it today such as optical drives and surgery equipment (Volti, 2014).

A final great example can be seen in the advent of computer science and the technology of early computation machines that brought that about. Though computer science is a relatively new study computational machinery has been around since at least 3000 B.C.E. when the Greeks developed complicated and intricate analog computational devices for tracking the movement of stars and planets (Shallit, 1995). This device may have been the first technological push in a long line of successive devices such as the difference engine which helped the science of mathematics to undertake ever more complex problems and eventually wind up in the creation of computer science at all. Perhaps without these early ventures in technological invention we would not have computers at all.

I personally believe that there will always be a need for technology to define and a drive for culture to fix the burdening issues with which we all must live. It is up to our ingenuity and creativity to mix the two in ways which can bring about the solutions to our problems and balance that power with the responsibility of what new consequences and burdens may be unveiled through its release into the world. We as problem solvers and developers shoulder that burden as much as we do the power it gives.
