The Computer

Probably one of the biggest innovation of the 1980’s and 1990’s the household computer is still in use today. Like many technological innovations, this innovation was one that consisted of a culmination of the many works before it and still continues its progression of advancement some thirty years later.

Computers were not entirely revolutionary by the time they were adopted for home use in 1981. There had been many examples of this technology out before then such as the TRS-80, Apple I & II, and the Programma 101. The real innovations in the hardware of the technology came with their arrival into the marketplace and the transition from kit-PCs marketed to mostly hobbyists and enthusiasts to a greater focus on the software and utilities. This led to such innovations as MS-Dos, the spread of PC gaming, and personal productivity software. As more and more everyday people started to purchase computers and use them for their work and play, the software grew to meet the demands and varied interests of its users. The line between a business computer and a personal computer blurred as home computers started adopting many of the tools of business computers such as local area networking (LAN), color graphics, and adopted similar system specifications.

The computer was such a success in the home market that in 1982 Time magazine named it “machine of the year”. This success continued to grow in tremendous strides and would not see signs of faltering until after 2010 when global PC sales started to level. This boon of technology in the home has greatly increased the productivity of its users tremendously. What would normally be reserved for only the biggest businesses or the most devoted hardware architecture students could be understood and utilized by everyone. This drop in the innovation pool of the 1980’s sent ripples that changed the climate for art, programming, business, and journalism; earning it its now often overlooked spot in history.
