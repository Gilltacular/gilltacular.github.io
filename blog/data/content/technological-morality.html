Technological Morality

Something I have said again and again when it comes to the morality of technology and its use remains true for this subject as well; Technology cannot be evil, it is on the user's shoulders whether and to what end they use the technology. Lets say for instance that I create an app that could kill people at the press of a button. Would the technology of the cellphone, or appstore software, or even the code used to write that be at fault? Would it be my idea coming to fruition that would be to blame? Was Albert Einstein a bad man for his help in creating the technology that was used in nuclear bombs? It is ultimately on the use of any technology by the user that makes it good or bad on the current socially accepted moral scale.

I find it both fascinating and horrific that we so easily shrug off the responsibility of creation on an inatimate object or idea. The Nissan Leaf for example, which features a system that tracks and rates drivers based on their economic impact in driving behaviors came under scrutiny for its ability to cause drivers to wreck into one another by promoting bad driving habits in the name of competitive advantage. It is much easier for us to lay the blame and responsibility of our action on what amount to just circuitry, software, and a screen. In reality these components did not cause the drivers to crash into each other by taking control of the wheel and favoring their ranking over their safety, the operators themselves made that choice. In comparison, every user must make their own choice when using technology; to use it for either what they deem to be good or they deem to be bad Whether that use be for social media or large scale weaponry makes no difference. It is on our decisions and the enforcement of those decisions as a collective society that determines what is/isn't acceptable and how something is/isn't used.

Designers have no responsibility or obligation to change the use of a technology because of what it might do. Instead those designers should be worried about making a quality product, without security holes, that releases on time, and best does what it was built to do. It is on us as a society to police each other and favor the governmental structure and laws that we think will best ensure our safety and success while protecting us from or removing the ability to use technology for evil. We should not be focusing on if designers are creating ethical products that inspire and influence us in a socially acceptable way, but instead focus on if we are holding ourselves personally and societally to the correct moral and life standards that would influence the use of such technology for the good. Limiting expansion, design, and research based on what technology could be is, in my opinion, a flawed philosophy that will only stagnate and hold us back in the long run. Instead we should look at the larger picture of ourselves in society. Are we in fact morally good and is society as a whole representative of the moral good we hold as personal moral and value? We should focus on bettering the communities around us and not stifling innovation to meet our desire to be faultless for the actions we take.

Science and technology are what we can do; morality is what we agree we should or should not do" ~ Edward Wilson

Persuasive technology has no bearing on the standing of what people become. We should all be filtering information for ourselves, doing our own research, and developing our own opinions and beliefs. Technology, once again, is simply a tool to allow this research and self exploration. Edward Wilson (2002), a Harvard professor and researcher, sums up this point clearly when he says, "Science and technology are what we can do; morality is what we agree we should or should not do".
