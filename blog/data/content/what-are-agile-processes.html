What are Agile Processes?

Agile processes are those processes that allow for the creation of software by building upon previous work towards the goal of a larger project. In this method of development, work is broken up into timed segments full of tasks to be completed. As stated in Essentials of Software Engineering (3rd Edition), the characteristics of Agile methods are:

“- Short releases and iterations: Divide the work into small pieces. Release the software to the customer as often as possible. - Incremental design: Don’t try to complete the design up front because not enough is known early about the system anyway. Delay design decisions as much as possible, and improve the existing design when more knowledge is acquired. - User involvement: Rather than trying to produce formal, complete, immutable standards at the beginning, ask the users involved with the project to provide constant feedback. This usually leads to a better-suited system. - Minimal documentation: Do only the necessary amount of documentation, which is just a means to an end. The source code is a big part of the actual documentation. - Informal communication: Maintain constant communication, but not necessarily through formal documents. People communicate better informally. This approach works as long as understanding is achieved. - Change: Assume that the requirements and environment will change, and try to find good ways to deal with this fact.” (Tsui et. al… 2014)

Agile development is geared toward working quickly in a team environment where communication and understanding are strong components of the group. In situations where these elements are not present established Agile methodologies may not work as well. For example, should the project be too large to break into small parts effectively within the time-frame needed for completion, or in an environment where developers do not work as part of a team or cultural/language barriers prevent effective collaboration between tasks, Agile development methodologies will not work as effectively.

In contrast, Agile development practices thrive in projects with easily identified goals that may fall under a simpler breadth than their traditional waterfall style counterparts and can provide a very efficient process for development in a very short time-frame. In addition, as stated in Essentials of Software Engineering (2014), “Because the customer is involved and the process deals well with changes, chances are the final product will be what the customers actually want when the project is completed rather than what was originally planned as requirements”. These traits make Agile development a strong choice for projects that meet its criteria and a powerful tool in the hands of project managers and developers.

Reference: Tsui, F., Karam, O., & Bernal, B. (2014). The Essentials of Software Engineering. Burlington, MA: Jones & Bartlett Learning
