##Part III: Guidelines for Healthy Discussion

In order to foster and maintain healthy discussions that further understanding of a topic all posts (including my own) should comply with the following guidelines.

  * Prepare yourself and your argument. Read the post you are commenting on and recent discussions before you add something. You don't want to repeat what someone else has said, but instead contribute to the total body of knowledge being developed.
  * Be sure to distinguish between your ideas and the work of others. When summarizing or quoting, always cite the source. If you are performing an analysis or personal opinion on research, use a signal phrase such as “in my opinion” to indicate a division between fact and your personal comments.
  * Avoid social networking and text messaging emoticons and abbreviations. Your discussion interactions should be given time and respect if you want your view or post to be treated as such. This means no “:)” or “lol”, etc.
  * Think before you post, then think again. Language is your only tool in an online discussion; there are no cues such as expression or body language to help convey your message. It is a good idea to re-read your post prior to hitting the submit button.
  * Criticism and debate are encouraged in postings as long as they are constructive, relevant, and well-articulated. Please be respectful of all views and comments.
  * No yelling in the discussions; this means no all-caps. If you want to emphasize something use bold, *asterisks*, or italicized type.
  * Always use proper writing style. Write as if you were preparing a term paper. Correct spelling, grammatical construction, and sentence structure are key to clarity and general understanding of your message.
