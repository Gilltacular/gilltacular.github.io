To analyze the strengths and weaknesses of quality assurance, quality control, and total quality management we must first understand what each of these practices are comprised of.

Quality Assurance (QA) is the effort to improve processes and planning towards the end goal of making the product better. As stated in Essentials of Software Engineering (3rd Edition), “Quality assurance refers to all activities designed to measure and improve quality in a product, including the whole process, training, and preparation of the team” (Tsui et. al… 2014).

Quality Control (QC) is the way in which quality of a product is measured. This is done through testing, reviews, formal methods, and static analysis. To ensure QC one must first define what quality looks like in their program. Quality can either take the form of conforming to predefined specifications or serving a predefined purpose (Tsui et. al… 2014). The measurement of that quality focus is then defined through the processes of verification and validation to ensure proper quality standards have been met.

Total Quality Management (TQM) is the effort of creating a permanent process and focus on the improvement of the ability to put out high quality products. This methodology varies greatly in implementation but tend to blend aspects of QC and QA together in its implementations.

These three techniques all aim to solve the problem of low quality product production and though they go about it with their own unique emphasis and process, each delivers on the end goal in a particular circumstance. There is not necessarily a best system for all cases but typically analyzing the product/customer needs will uncover which is best suited to a particular project.

Reference:

Tsui, F., Karam, O., & Bernal, B. (2014). The Essentials of Software Engineering. Burlington, MA: Jones & Bartlett Learning
