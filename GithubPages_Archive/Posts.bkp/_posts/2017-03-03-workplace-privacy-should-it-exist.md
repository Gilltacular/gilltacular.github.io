I believe that electronic monitoring needs to be clearly defined before we proceed with an analysis of this issue. With a strong understanding of the elements we should be able to arrive at a concrete conclusion about the rights of an individual in a particular circumstance. With an assumption that the definition of electronically monitoring an employee entails simply the evaluation, gathering of data, and assessment of said employee’s work results and process, it stands to reason that employers have just cause to electronically monitor their employees.

It is important for businesses to measure the effectiveness of processes and results from all elements within itself. One of the greater portions that make up many businesses today is that of the human workforce. Some would even classify this as perhaps one of the most difficult to measure and predict elements of those essential to everyday operation. However, just as we would not scoff at providing access to records of a machine’s performance and ability, we should not take issue with the same being done for the human element either. In fact studies such as the those conducted on the Hawthorne Effect seem to point to the idea that simply being monitored increases the effectiveness and attentiveness of employees (McCarney, Warner, … & Fisher, 2007).

The real issues start to take rise when this monitoring is abused for any purpose outside the scope of employee evaluation and work performance. When constrained to the scope of work performance, goal creation, and employee assessment, the metrics hold merit. The collection of this data, the chain of custody, its contents, and eventual reporting must be transparent and thoroughly vetted in order for those subject to it to be comfortable with and trust those who manage it. I also believe that such data should be used without regard for position as well. If you are going to subject some element of your company to such monitoring you cannot exclude yourself or a chosen element from similar monitoring as well or you risk creating animosity for the system and those excluded. In my opinion, it is only through equal, fair, and transparent processes that electronically monitoring of employees can be realized and used to its greatest potential.

It is clear that not every company will adhere to the same strict code of ethics and fairness in the use of data collected. Due to the same elements that would require monitoring of employees, such data is at risk to exposure to the abuse of the data collected. This has been realized in everything from company leaks, insider trading, market manipulation and recent improper security implementations. I feel that the value of such monitoring is currently at a disadvantage and must present more benefit to the individual employee’s performance, safety, and general quality of life to win credibility once again.

Government regulation is one way in which companies could be held liable and responsible for how data collected through electronic monitoring is handled. This effort would need more than the mere creation of laws and regulation though as laws themselves have proven ineffective in the culling of the elements they mean to regulate at an absolute level. It also stands to reason that such regulatory audits or monitoring would itself need to be held to the same standards which they enforce which could present an infinite loop of recursive monitoring of the monitors who monitor the monitors who monitor…. you get the idea.

Another way to enforce best practices would be to build a framework with which the utilization of processed data could be kept consistently protected and away from access from any until the end report was made. Such a framework could itself be vetted and maintained regularly in the same way that a regulatory commission would need to be audited. The problems with this approach is that it does not wholly eliminate the potential for misuse but instead narrows the scope of those who are responsible and in turn those who should be held accountable should abuse of sensitive information occur.

The topic is an interesting one with many solutions and many still yet to be realized in lieu of the adoption of such a widespread monitoring accountability promise by companies and organizations taking part in the practice. The future is the only true test of what will be.
